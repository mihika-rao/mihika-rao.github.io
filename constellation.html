Constellation

Notes usually disappear into folders. I wanted to see what happens if the interface is organized around relationships instead: ideas become nodes, shared concepts become edges, and searching becomes a way to see the shape of what you’re thinking about.

WHY BUILD IT?

My own tabs became the user research.

I had spent years finding relationships in astronomical data while my own notes lived in an archaeological dig of folders, screenshots, papers, and half-remembered tabs.

Constellation is deliberately small: a working interaction prototype, not a fake claim that I built a production knowledge platform. It tests one idea — whether showing relationships can make personal information easier to rediscover.

Prototype logic is intentionally transparent: simple token overlap, no hidden AI API. The point here is the interaction model.

Edges appear when two notes share enough meaningful terms. Search dims unrelated nodes so clusters become easier to see.

Not “ask AI a question.” More like: show me what I keep circling back to.

HOW IT WORKS

A tiny semantic layer.

01Write a note
02Normalize words
03Remove common terms
04Score overlap
05Draw relationships

The prototype is intentionally local and explainable. A production version could swap the overlap score for embeddings, preserve provenance, and use an LLM for controlled labeling/synthesis.

WHAT I’D TEST NEXT

Does seeing a relationship actually help?

I’d measure whether the graph improves rediscovery compared with folders/search alone: time-to-retrieve an old note, successful resurfacing after 7/30 days, whether suggested connections are accepted, and whether people return to clusters rather than individual documents.

That is the part I care about most: turning a playful interface idea into a measurable product hypothesis.
