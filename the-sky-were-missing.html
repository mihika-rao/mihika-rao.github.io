The sky we’re missing

Where does artificial light obscure the Milky Way—and how much of each country does that cover?

Geospatial analysisPythonAstronomy

THE WORK BEHIND THE PICTURE

Follow the question.

What I did

I classified the World Atlas’s raw sky-brightness cells using its Milky Way visibility cutoff, clipped them to country boundaries, and weighted each cell by its geographic area. The denominator is mapped area throughout; no population data are used.

What stood out

In this eight-country selection, about half of Belgium’s mapped area lies above the cutoff, compared with about 3% of Switzerland’s. The maps show where those areas fall, rather than using decorative shapes to stand in for geography.

What this can’t tell us

These are historical modeled estimates from 2014 satellite inputs, published in 2016. The eight countries are a selected comparison, not a Europe-wide ranking. Observing conditions affect real visibility. Maps are resized independently; compare the percentages, not silhouette size.

Sources & method

Cutoff: artificial zenith brightness >0.688 mcd/m². Native-resolution cells are area-weighted on the WGS84 ellipsoid. European territories only; generalized Natural Earth boundaries. Boundary inclusion changes every result by less than 0.4 percentage points. Full cutoff sensitivity and geographic scope are in the reproducibility files.
