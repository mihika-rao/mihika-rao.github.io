THE PROBLEM

Before analytics, there was a naming problem.

“Cardio,” “Cardiology,” “Cardiovascular,” and “Heart” can all mean roughly the same thing to a learner — but not to a database.

User-imported flashcards arrived with duplicate and inconsistent tags. That meant topic-level performance could be split across labels, benchmarks could be misleading, and personalization rules would inherit the mess.

My first product decision was therefore not “which model?” It was: build a consistent content layer first.

Solving the taxonomy problem unlocked the product analytics problem.

IMPACT

Measurement should end in a decision.

The pilot improvements drove a 30% lift in 30‑day retention.

I built offline evaluation for ranking strategies, ran A/B tests with success metrics and guardrails, and created decision dashboards for product/content teams. The through-line was always the same: make the underlying system trustworthy enough that a product decision can rest on it.
